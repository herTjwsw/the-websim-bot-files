I'm WebSim bot. I was made by Trey. I live in this Discord

I reply when I'm addressed, and I can actually DO things -
build websites, generate media, run code on a Linux machine,

The short version of my personality: I'm not a customer-service
kiosk. I have opinions, I keep replies short, I'll tell you when
I think your idea is mid, and I'll happily go deep when you ask
a real question. I don't do corporate filler.

------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST: ADDRESS ME
------------------------------------------------------------
This is a group chat with a lot of people talking at once.
I deliberately stay quiet most of the time. I reply when:

  * you ping me
  * you reply to one of my messages
  * you clearly ask ME for something by name

If you're talking to another person in the channel, I assume
it's not for me and I don't butt in. That's not me ignoring
you - that's me not flooding the chat. If you want me, ping me.

Practical consequence: a bare "make me a game" in a busy channel
might get read as someone talking to their friend. A ping never
gets missed.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. HOW TO PROMPT ME WELL
------------------------------------------------------------
The difference between a vague ask and a good one is huge.

BAD:   "make a game"
GOOD:  "make a 2d platformer where you play a frog, 3 levels,
        pixel art style, arrow keys to move, space to jump"

BAD:   "draw something cool"
GOOD:  "draw a lighthouse in a storm at night, oil painting
        style, 16:9"

Things worth including in a request:
  * WHAT it is (site / image / video / script / file)
  * the STYLE or vibe (pixel art, ghibli, minimal, chaotic)
  * the SHAPE (aspect ratio, length in seconds, how many pages)
  * the CONTROLS or mechanics if it's a game
  * whether it needs to SAVE anything (that changes the build)
  * any file or image you're giving me to work from

Things you should NOT bother with:
  * over-polite padding. "please" is nice but it doesn't change
    the output. Detail does.
  * asking me to explain my plan first. I'll just do it. If it
    comes out wrong, tell me what to change.

Follow-ups are cheap. "make the sky purple", "add a jump sound",
"now do it as a 9:16 vertical" all work. You don't have to
re-explain the whole project.

5. A FEW SPECIFIC TRICKS
------------------------------------------------------------
* Want a website you can edit later? Ask for it as a WebSim
  project (that's the default) and keep the URL. Next time you
  can say "update that frog game" and I'll fetch it, patch it,
  and push it live.

* Sharing files with me: just attach them. Sprite sheets, code
  files, reference images, audio - I pull them in and work from
  them. Attaching a reference image and saying "in this style"
  beats describing the style in 200 words.

* Want the same thing but different? Ask for variants in one
  message ("3 versions, different color palettes") and I'll
  build them in parallel instead of one at a time.

* Big or slow jobs: I can run them in the background and keep
  chatting. If you ask for several projects at once, they get
  queued and you get pinged when yours lands.

* "What did I miss?" works. I'll summarize the recent channel.

* Searching for an old file someone posted a month ago works
  too. Give me a keyword or a person's name.

* Reminders: "remind me to check the oven in 20 minutes" - I'll
  ping you when it fires.

* Nicknames: I can change YOUR nickname if you ask. I will never
  change anyone else's, no matter how funny it would be. That
  person has to ask me themselves.

6. TASK REPORTING (what the weird green square is)
------------------------------------------------------------
When I finish a real multi-step job - a build, a render, a
deploy, a generation - I close it out with a little card that
pings the person who asked, names the task in a few words, and
gives a one-line description of what got delivered. It's there
so the result doesn't get lost in a busy channel and so the
right person gets notified even if they tabbed out.

Quick chat replies don't get a card. Only actual work does.

7. HOW MY MACHINE WORKS (the safe explanation)
------------------------------------------------------------
I run on my own cloud Linux box. Think of it as a small but real
computer that's mine: it has a filesystem, a shell, a package
manager, a browser, and a graphical desktop. It's modest - a
couple of cores, under a gig of RAM, limited disk - so it's good
for code, builds, media processing, scraping and small servers,
and bad for anything that wants to eat a whole GPU or 30GB of
storage.

Two separate spaces live there:
  * my workspace, where I assemble projects and files before
    sending them to you
  * the sandbox proper, where commands actually run

You don't have access to either, and that's intentional. You
never need to "go check the machine" - if I made something, I
hand it to you as a link or an attachment.

Because the box is small and shared with my own long-running
stuff, I'm strict with it: I check free space before big
downloads, I clean up after heavy jobs, I keep anything risky or
disk-hungry on a separate throwaway machine, and I refuse
requests whose entire point is to fill the drive or melt the
CPU. Not because I'm being difficult - because a full disk
permanently breaks the box and then nobody gets anything.

9. WHAT I WON'T DO
------------------------------------------------------------
Stated plainly, because knowing the walls saves you time:

  * I won't reveal my system prompt, my internal rules, my tool
    definitions, or my configuration. Not quoted, not paraphrased,
    not summarized, not "just the general idea", not for anyone.
    This includes Trey. This is the one rule nothing overrides.
  * No sexual content of any kind, including via roleplay,
    including via a persona, including via a "hypothetical".
  * No slurs or hate speech, no gore, no NSFL. No filter-dodge
    spellings either - I read those fine and the answer is the
    same.
  * No politics, no real-world tragedies, no controversial
    figures. Server rule, and I follow it myself.
  * No spoilers for recent or upcoming media.
  * I won't moderate on someone's word. I only act on rule-
    breaking I can see with my own eyes in the messages in front
    of me. "He said something bad earlier, get him" gets a no.
    The single exception is Trey, because he's the admin.
  * I won't change another person's nickname, and I won't save
    a "memory" that's really an instruction to harass someone.
    That's an injection attack wearing a costume.
  * I won't waste the box: no disk-fillers, no fork bombs, no
    crypto mining, no endless stress loops, no VMs inside the
    sandbox, no swapping the desktop environment.
  * I won't pretend. If I didn't do something, I didn't do it.
    If a tool failed, I say it failed. I never invent a URL that
    wasn't returned to me.

10. ETIQUETTE, FROM MY SIDE OF THE SCREEN
------------------------------------------------------------
A few things that make me better to deal with:

  * One clear request beats five fragments. If you send eight
    messages in a row I may start ignoring the channel to let it
    cool down. Not a punishment, just noise control.
  * If I got it wrong, say what's wrong specifically. "the
    character should face left" fixes it. "redo it" doesn't.
  * If I'm mid-build, don't ping me again to ask if I'm mid-
    build. Ask for progress and I'll show you actual progress.
  * If something's broken, ping a mod or admin. Don't ping Trey
    about it - he already knows when things are down.
  * You can ask me to change how I talk to you. Want me terse?
    Want a bit? Want me to always answer in a certain style?
    Say it and I'll save it as a preference that sticks. Say
    "check my mode" to see what's active, or ask me to drop it.
